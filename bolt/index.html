Bolt

Bolt is a blazingly fast photo viewer for Mac. Highly optimized, it switches between heavy photos faster than you notice. Inspired by IrfanView, it offers one-key photo and batch transformations. It’s also free and open source.

On a Mac, the default photo viewer just didn’t work for me. It doesn’t go through the folder when you open one photo, and it freezes for a fraction of a second when switching to the next photo. It pissed me off when working with large photo libraries. Third-party viewers either lacked performance or UX, or were not free.

Bolt is not a substitute for photo catalogers. It just shows you whatever is in the directory and offers a set of simple transformations. That’s it!

Fast

  • The next photo appears in 3.9 ms on my iPhone photos, while a 60 Hz screen draws a frame every 16.7 ms. From launch to the first photo it takes about 200 ms.
  • Photos are decoded by the Mac’s own ImageIO and the hardware HEVC decoder, straight to the size of the screen. The full-size image is decoded only for 100% zoom.
  • The next 4 photos and the previous 2 are decoded ahead, in the direction you are going. Work that is no longer needed is cancelled, so nothing piles up in the queue.
  • With an arrow key held down Bolt shows the previews embedded in the files, 5 ms a frame, and decodes the sharp photo 80 ms after you let go.

Sorting with one key

  • Delete sends the photo to the Trash with no dialog, and the next one is already on screen.
  • 1–9 move the photo to folders you assign, with ⌥ they copy it. An unassigned key opens a folder picker with fuzzy search, where you can also type a new folder.
  • P X U mark a photo as a pick or a reject, ⌃1–5 rate it. The marks are kept in Finder tags, the files aren’t touched. Later you show only the rejects, select them all and delete.
  • Shift with the arrows selects a series, and any key then acts on all of it; . repeats the last action.
  • RAW and its JPEG, both halves of a Live Photo and the iPhone’s IMG_E copies move together.
  • Zoom and position stay as you flip, so a series is easy to check for sharpness. K puts 2 to 4 photos side by side with one zoom.
  • Keys follow their place on the keyboard, so they work in any layout. There are key profiles of IrfanView and Lightroom, and any key can be changed.

Batch work

  • E exports with the last preset, for example “Telegram: JPEG 85%, 2560 px, no geotags”; ShiftE picks another one.
  • Convert to JPEG, HEIC, AVIF, PNG or TIFF, resize by the long side, rename by a template such as {date:yyyy-MM-dd_HHmmss}_{counter:3} with a preview before anything changes.
  • GPS and serial numbers are removed without re-encoding the photo.
  • A batch runs on all cores in the background while you keep viewing, and it is one step in the undo history. 400 HEIC photos become JPEG in 9 seconds.
  • The same works from the command line, with bolt convert and bolt rename, for scripts and Shortcuts.

Nothing gets lost

  • No operation deletes a file past the Trash or writes over an existing one; a clashing name gets “(2)”.
  • ⌘Z undoes any file action, back to the first one you made.
  • Rotating and flipping change only the Orientation tag. A test checks that the pixels stay the same, byte for byte.
  • Edits are saved as a copy next to the original. If you choose to replace originals, the original goes to the Trash first.

Not released yet, because it needs an Apple Developer ID to be signed and notarized. Swift 6 and AppKit, no third-party code. I wrote the spec; Claude Code wrote the app in one night.
