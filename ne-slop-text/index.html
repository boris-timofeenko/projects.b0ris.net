ne-slop-text

A Claude Code skill that writes a text from your own material and then edits only the sentences that three default models would write too.

Install ▶Source on GitHub

I made a quest game, Belgrade 2026, and its graphics came out alive: photos from my phone ruined by a script, characters drawn in code, a hero cut out of a friend’s photo with the white fringe of the magic eraser left on. That became my ne-slop skill for creative work. For text it was not enough, and text run through the popular humanizer skill I could spot from a mile away.

Humanizer and skills like it work from lists of words and patterns. The text loses “delve” and the em dashes but keeps the skeleton a model gives it: the same opening, the same lesson at the end. ne-slop-text starts from the other end. It finds where models land by default for this exact task and moves the draft away from there.

What it does

  • Writes from your material, not from the task alone: notes, a CV, a job ad, and best of all a transcript of you answering a couple of questions out loud, in any language.
  • Puts down a skeleton of 3 to 5 beats first. Every later edit has to keep them, so the story doesn’t fall apart into a list of facts.
  • Has three fresh agents on Opus, Sonnet and Haiku write the same task with no method. These are the defaults, the place where a model lands by itself.
  • Marks what the draft takes from all three defaults at once: the same opening, the same moral, the same figure from Google.
  • Edits only those sentences, and edits rather than deletes. A checker makes sure no fact got blurred and the beats are still there; a reader agent makes sure the text is still clear.
  • Asks a guard one question: is the draft still in the machine cloud of its task? The number is never the goal; why is below.
  • Finishes with a surface lint in English and Russian, built on the word and pattern lists of humanizer and unslop.
  • Gives back the text, a few lines on what changed and why, and the questions your material couldn’t answer. Your files stay untouched.

One paragraph, three ways

How I tested it

I built it with Claude on my own texts, a CV and letters to employers, and tested it blind on myself. The test page shuffles the variants and evens out the typography, because once a human original was the only text with an em dash, and I put it last.

Version 1 fixed only what the draft shared with all three defaults. In a letter to a hiring team it moved the draft from third place to first. The two Opus letters in that test had the same skeleton down to the closing line, “I’d be glad to have that coffee”.

Version 2 started with a night experiment. Three agents collected 139 texts by 37 talented authors written before 2022 and rebuilt the task behind each, and Opus, Sonnet and Haiku wrote every task too: 417 defaults. A model learned which sentence moves separate the two sides. On the model side, glosses, morals, signposts and recap endings. On the human side, asides, questions, irony and quoted speech.

A pair is one human text and one default for the same task; the metric is right when it puts the human one further from the machine side.

Then the loop drove texts down that scale, and they got worse. The metric’s order matched mine about as often as chance, and three times its top pick was my last. The reviser had cut every link and frame, and a story became a list of facts. What worked was structure: the beats that every edit keeps, edits instead of deletions, the reader’s clarity floor and the truth check. With those the new loop came second on both of the last two pages and never last. The metric stayed only as the guard.

How to use it

git clone https://github.com/boris-timofeenko/ne-slop-text ~/.claude/skills/ne-slop-text

Then ask Claude Code to write or fix a text “ne-slop”, or run /ne-slop-text with the task and whatever material you have:

/ne-slop-text write a message to the hiring team for this job: <link>. My CV is cv.pdf. What I said about the project: <transcript>
/ne-slop-text fix this paragraph: <text>. What actually happened: <notes>

Texts under about 120 words skip the loop. Longer ones take 14 to 18 agent calls and 10 to 15 minutes. The scripts need Python 3 and nothing else.

One judge, a handful of tasks, mostly about work, so the numbers show a direction. Every round, including what failed, is in docs/evidence.md.
